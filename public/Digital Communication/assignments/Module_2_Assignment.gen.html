Objective

Derive rather than memorise the error probability of each scheme, and defend a modulation choice against a stated set of link constraints.

Learning outcomes

  • Student will be able to draw the constellation of any scheme in this module and compute its minimum distance in terms of energy per bit.
  • Student will be able to derive error probabilities from geometry rather than quoting formulae.
  • Student will be able to quantify the penalty of noncoherent and differentially coherent detection.
  • Student will be able to select a modulation scheme given bandwidth, power, phase stability and amplifier constraints.

Part A — Problems (17 marks)

P1. Draw the constellations of BPSK, QPSK, 8-PSK and 16-QAM to a common scale at equal average energy E. Tabulate the minimum distance of each as a multiple of sqrt(E) and as a multiple of sqrt(Eb), and explain why the two orderings differ. [CO2 | L4 | 3 marks]

P2. Derive the bit error probability of coherent BPSK starting from the Gaussian density of the noise projection. State every assumption used and identify which one fails if the channel is fading. [CO2 | L4 | 3 marks]

P3. For 16-QAM at Eb/N0 = 15 dB, compute the symbol error probability and the bit error probability under Gray mapping. Then compute the required Eb/N0 for 16-PSK to reach the same bit error rate and state the difference in decibels. [CO2 | L4 | 3 marks]

P4. A BFSK system uses Tb = 200 microseconds. Compute the minimum frequency separation for coherent and for noncoherent orthogonality, and the resulting occupied bandwidth in each case. Comment on which cost dominates at this bit rate. [CO2 | L3 | 2 marks]

P5. Differentially encode the bit sequence 1 0 1 1 0 0 1 with an initial reference phase of 0 degrees. Introduce a single symbol error at position 4 and show precisely which decoded bits are corrupted and why. [CO2 | L4 | 2 marks]

P6. Plot, by hand or by computation, the bit error rate against Eb/N0 from 0 to 15 dB for BPSK, coherent BFSK, noncoherent BFSK and DPSK on one set of semi-logarithmic axes. Mark the required Eb/N0 for each at Pb = 1e-5 and annotate the gaps in decibels. [CO2 | L5 | 1 marks]

P7. A satellite downlink offers 36 MHz of bandwidth, needs 60 Mbit/s and has a received Eb/N0 of 12.5 dB into a travelling-wave-tube amplifier that must run saturated. Recommend a modulation scheme, show that it meets both constraints, and state what would have to change for 64-QAM to become viable. [CO2 | L5 | 2 marks]

P8. Explain, with a sketch of the constellation, what a carrier phase error of 15 degrees does to a QPSK receiver, and compute the resulting increase in bit error rate at Eb/N0 = 10 dB. [CO2 | L5 | 1 marks]

Part B — Theory (3 marks)

T1. Argue whether the 3 dB penalty of orthogonal signalling relative to antipodal signalling is a property of FSK specifically or of orthogonality in general. Support your answer with a scheme other than FSK. [CO2 | L4 | 1 marks]

T2. Modern systems increasingly use high-order QAM despite the amplifier back-off. Identify what changed in the technology to make that trade favourable, and what would reverse it. [CO2 | L5 | 1 marks]

T3. Gray mapping leaves the constellation geometry untouched yet improves the bit error rate. Explain why this is not a free lunch in disguise, and identify the regime where its benefit disappears. [CO2 | L4 | 1 marks]
