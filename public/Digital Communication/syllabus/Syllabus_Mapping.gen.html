Provenance

Built from TB1 Sections 2.8-2.11 and 7.1-7.4, the sections the syllabus names. The organising idea is a single change of representation: a bandpass waveform carries no information in its carrier, so the carrier is removed analytically to leave a complex lowpass envelope, and that envelope is then projected onto an orthonormal basis so that a continuous waveform becomes a point in a finite-dimensional space. Once signals are points and noise is a Gaussian cloud, the optimum receiver is forced rather than invented: it is nearest-neighbour decision, and the correlation receiver and matched filter are two circuits computing the same projection.

Provenance

Built from TB1 Sections 7.5-7.8 and 7.10-7.14, which the syllabus names. Every scheme in this module is the same three questions asked again: where are the constellation points, what is the minimum distance in terms of energy per bit, and what does the receiver need to know about the carrier. The unifying result is that error probability always takes the form Q of a distance divided by the noise spread, so once a constellation is drawn its performance follows without further integration. Noncoherent detection is introduced at the end as the price paid when phase is unavailable, which sets up the trade-off between receiver simplicity and about 1 dB of energy.

Provenance

Built from TB1 Sections 5.1-5.10, which the syllabus names. The module has two halves that mirror each other. The source-coding half asks how few bits a source needs and answers with entropy, achieved by Huffman and Lempel-Ziv. The channel-coding half asks how many bits a channel can carry and answers with capacity, the maximum of mutual information over input distributions. Both are existence results: they say what is possible without saying how, which is exactly the gap that Modules 4 and 5 fill. The Information Capacity Law at the end unifies them and produces the Shannon limit of -1.6 dB, the number every coding scheme is ultimately measured against.

Provenance

Built from TB1 Sections 10.1-10.5, which the syllabus names. The module is the constructive answer to Module 3: capacity said good codes exist, and this is the first family that can actually be built and decoded. The organising idea is linear algebra over GF(2) — codewords form a subspace, the generator matrix is its basis, the parity check matrix annihilates it, and the syndrome is the image of the received word under that annihilator. Everything about detection and correction capability follows from one number, the minimum distance. Cyclic codes then add structure so that matrix multiplication becomes polynomial arithmetic and the encoder collapses to a shift register.

Provenance

Built from TB1 Sections 10.6-10.8, which the syllabus names. The shift from Module 4 is from blocks to streams: a convolutional encoder has memory, so each output depends on several past inputs and there is no block boundary to align to. The same encoder admits three equivalent pictures — tree, trellis and state diagram — and the module spends time on why the trellis is the useful one: it is the only one that does not grow with sequence length, which is precisely what makes the Viterbi algorithm linear rather than exponential. Free distance replaces minimum distance as the figure of merit, and the module closes on hard versus soft decision decoding, where 2 dB is available for the cost of keeping more than one bit from the demodulator.
